I am a product engineer in İstanbul. I started on the front end: HTML, SCSS,
then Vue, then React, and kept walking backwards into the parts that decide
whether a product survives: the types at the edges, the deploy, the one field
name that is wrong only at runtime.

In 2026 that became [koative](https://koative.com), an independent software
studio. It ships its own products first and takes a few client projects a year,
which is the arrangement that keeps the judgement honest: nobody else's
deadline decides what our own app is allowed to be. The first product is
[Lumi](https://koative.com/lumi/), an iOS app built around a single daily
ritual rather than a dashboard.

My bias is toward less: fewer screens, fewer dependencies, fewer settings
nobody will ever open. I would rather ship a boring service that survives a
year unattended than a clever one that needs me awake. Most of my writing is an
argument with a younger version of myself who thought clever was the point.

## Rules I work by

- Types live at the edges. A typo in a field name should fail `tsc`, not
  production.
- One artifact to deploy. If it takes a runbook, it is not finished.
- Every dependency must justify its own line in the lockfile.
- Ship less, then spend the saved time on the details people actually feel.

## Working together

koative takes on a few client projects a year: iOS and Android apps, web
platforms, and the product design in between. Best fit: a team that would
rather cut scope than cut care.
