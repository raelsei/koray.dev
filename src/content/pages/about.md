I am a product engineer who ended up in fintech and stayed, because money is the
one domain where being wrong is unambiguous. Wrong number, wrong balance, angry
customer. That constraint made me a better engineer than any framework did.

I spent six years inside payments teams — reconciliation, ledgers, KYC flows, the
parts of a product that never get a launch post. Then large language models
arrived and made a specific kind of work possible: software that reads messy
human financial mess and turns it into something structured enough to act on.
That is what Koative builds.

My bias is toward small: small teams, small dependency lists, small pull
requests. I would rather ship a boring service that survives a year unattended
than a clever one that needs me awake. Most of my writing is an argument with a
younger version of myself who thought clever was the point.

Outside of work: long walks along the Bosphorus, filter coffee measured to the
gram, and a bookshelf of half-read systems books I keep promising to finish.

## Rules I work by

- Money is stored in minor units, as integers. Always.
- A model may draft intent. Only the ledger commits it.
- Every dependency must justify its own line in the lockfile.
- If it cannot run unattended for a year, it is not finished.
